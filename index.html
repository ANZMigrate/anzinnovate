National Innovation visa / subclass 858

Permanent residence for exceptional talent.

The National Innovation visa (NIV), subclass 858, is a permanent Australian visa for people with an internationally recognised record of exceptional and outstanding achievement in a profession, a sport, the arts, or academia and research. It is intended for senior professionals and technical leaders, senior academics and researchers, entrepreneurs, innovative investors, athletes and creatives who are genuinely among the best in their field and whose work would benefit the Australian community.

Why the NIV is different

No points test, no occupation list, no sponsor.

The Department of Home Affairs (the Department) assesses the NIV on the applicant's record of achievement. Age, qualifications and work experience earn no points, and no skilled occupation list applies. The two questions that matter are whether that record places the applicant among the best in the field internationally, and whether the applicant would be an asset to the Australian community.

PROutcome

Permanent on grant

The visa is permanent on grant and carries a five-year travel facility. For citizenship purposes, permanent residence starts on the day of grant if the holder is in Australia, or on first entry to Australia on this visa if granted offshore.

  • Live, work and study anywhere in Australia
  • Medicare and family sponsorship rights (waiting periods apply to some government payments)
0Job offer needed

No employer sponsorship

The NIV requires neither an Australian employer sponsor nor a job offer. Applicants must, however, show that they could find work, or become established, in their field in Australia without difficulty.

  • Unrestricted work rights
  • Open to founders, researchers and senior executives
AnyAge

No age limit

Applicants of any age may apply. Those under 18 or aged 55 or over when the visa application is made must also satisfy the Department that they would be of exceptional benefit to the Australian community.

  • The benefit must be ongoing, sustained and have the potential to be realised shortly after migration
  • Evidence may include a senior role or a business plan
ENEnglish

No English test score

Applicants aged 18 or over must either show functional English or pay the second instalment of the visa application charge in place of that evidence.

  • No IELTS band score or points threshold
  • Second instalment payable before grant
+1Family

Family included

A partner and dependent children may be included in the application, subject to health and character requirements.

  • Adult applicants may add family members before a decision; an applicant under 18 may include only a parent and that parent's family unit, and only at lodgement
  • Children aged 18 to 22 must be dependent
  • An additional charge applies for each family member
In/OutLocation

Apply onshore or offshore

Applicants may be in or outside Australia both at lodgement and at grant, provided they are not in immigration clearance.

  • Onshore applicants must hold a substantive visa or a Bridging visa A, B or C. Submitting an EOI does not give a bridging visa
  • Applicants who hold, or last held, a subclass 491 or 494 visa must have held it for at least three years

Core requirements

An internationally recognised record, recent and continuing.

The legal test is demanding. The applicant's record must be internationally recognised in a profession, a sport, the arts, or academia and research, and the achievements must be recent enough to show that the applicant remains active and prominent in that field. A successful career alone does not meet the standard. A senior role may be considered where the organisation, and the applicant's work within it, are recognised on the international stage.

The NIV is not a general skilled migration visa. It is reserved for people who are genuinely among the best in their field, recognised internationally, and working in one of Australia's designated priority sectors. For professionals, this usually means a senior technical expert at an internationally competitive organisation, or a senior leader or executive at an internationally reputed organisation. In the Education sector, it means Professors and Associate Professors (or the international equivalent) and senior university leaders, not entry-level or mid-career researchers.

What the Department must be satisfied of

The applicant's achievements place them among the best in the field.

The Department must be satisfied that the applicant has superior abilities and achievements compared with others in the field, is acclaimed as exceptional wherever that field is practised, and has a sustained record of achievement that is unlikely to diminish in the near future.

  • An internationally recognised record of exceptional and outstanding achievement
  • Recent achievements showing the applicant remains active and prominent in the field
  • A field that is recognised in Australia and has international standing
  • A contribution to the nation as a whole: economic, social, cultural, or raising Australia's academic, artistic or sporting standing internationally
  • The ability to find work, or become independently established, in the area of achievement in Australia without difficulty
  • Nomination on Form 1000 by an Australian citizen, permanent resident, eligible New Zealand citizen or Australian organisation with a national reputation in the same area
  • Health, character and functional English requirements (or payment of the second instalment)

Indicators of exceptional achievement

The indicators in Ministerial Direction 120 and Home Affairs guidance.

Ministerial Direction 120 sets out the indicators the Department considers when assessing whether an applicant's achievement is exceptional and outstanding. The Direction describes them as guidance, and each case is decided on its overall merits. We expect at least two indicators of exceptional achievement, presented together as a clear picture of sustained, internationally recognised achievement.

01Top of field

International awards

Awards that place the recipient at the top of the discipline worldwide, such as a Nobel Prize, Fields Medal, Turing Award, Pulitzer Prize or Olympic gold medal.

  • Highest priority for invitation
  • Not limited to a priority sector
02Research funding

National research grants

National-level research grants, or equivalent grants from other countries, that indicate top-of-field standing, such as Australian Research Council grants, Department of Education Accelerator grants, UKRI grants, European Commission funding or US National Science Foundation funding.

  • The grant must have been awarded to the applicant, for example as a lead or named investigator
  • The grant must be national-level or an international equivalent; internal institutional funding is not at that level
03Academic influence

PhD with thought leadership

A PhD combined with demonstrated influence in the field, shown through recent publications in top-ranked journals, an h-index that is high for the applicant's career stage, or a research degree from a university ranked in the top 100.

  • Early career benchmark: an h-index of 14 or higher (higher at later career stages)
  • Times Higher Education World University Rankings used as the guide to the top 100
  • Recent articles published in respected and highly ranked journals
04Recognition

Keynote appearances

Recent keynote or invited speaking roles at high-profile international conferences in the applicant's field, such as Web Summit, the International Congress of Mathematicians or the AACR Annual Meeting.

  • Invited keynote or featured speaker roles, not routine submitted paper presentations
  • External industry or academic events only; internal company events are not counted
05Innovation

Patents and intellectual property

Recognised intellectual property attributed to the applicant, such as international patents for new products or technology relevant to the field of achievement.

  • The applicant must be a named inventor or co-inventor
  • Granted patents carry the most weight
06Earnings

High income

Current earnings, or a written offer of employment in Australia, at or above the Fair Work high income threshold, earned in the applicant's area of achievement. The threshold is AUD 190,100 from 1 July 2026 and is indexed each 1 July.

  • Measured as guaranteed annual earnings (base salary plus guaranteed amounts such as packaged benefits), excluding superannuation, discretionary bonuses and commissions
  • Income earned outside the field of achievement is not counted
07Technical leadership

Senior technical role

A senior technical role in a priority sector at an organisation that competes and is recognised on the international stage.

  • The organisation's international standing matters
  • Evidence of the applicant's own impact strengthens the claim
08Senior management

Organisational leadership

Leading or directing an internationally reputed organisation in a priority sector.

  • The organisation's standing and the applicant's role both matter
  • Applies to executives and senior leaders, not middle management
09Academia

Senior academic appointment

A current Professor or Associate Professor appointment (Australian Academic Level D or E, or the international equivalent) at a well-regarded institution.

  • Entry-level and mid-career academic roles do not meet this indicator
  • Usually combined with publications, h-index and grants

Other evidence the Department may consider includes advisory board appointments, statements from peak professional bodies, and international media coverage of the applicant's work.

Priority sectors

Invitation priority follows the sector.

The Department issues invitations in priority order. Top-of-field award holders come first, then candidates nominated by an expert government agency. Candidates with exceptional achievement in a Tier One sector follow, and then those in a Tier Two sector.

Priority 3 / Tier One

Critical Technologies, Health Industries, Renewables and Low Emission Technologies

Explore Tier One specialisations

This tier covers AI, robotics and autonomous systems; cyber security and quantum; biotechnology and clean energy; advanced manufacturing and materials; communications, photonics and sensing; health industries, including genomics, precision medicine, AI in health and implantable and wearable devices; and renewables and low emission technologies, including renewable hydrogen, green metals, carbon storage and critical minerals processing.

Our assessment benchmark for Tier One is at least two indicators of exceptional achievement, supported by evidence of achievement in the sector.

Priority 4 / Tier Two

Agri-food and AgTech, Defence Capabilities and Space, Education, Financial Services and FinTech, Infrastructure and Transport, Resources

Explore Tier Two specialisations

This tier covers agricultural biotechnology, food technology and biosecurity; defence inputs, electromagnetic technologies, propulsion and space systems engineering; senior university management and Professor or Associate Professor appointments (Academic Level D or E, or the international equivalent); blockchain, cloud technologies, software engineering and financial data science; innovative transport manufacturing and large transport infrastructure projects; and automation, geology, metallurgy, beneficiation and critical minerals value-adding in resources.

As for Tier One, our assessment benchmark for Tier Two is at least two indicators relevant to the sector, each supported by evidence of achievement.

Education sector

The Education sector is for senior academics and institutional leaders, not entry-level or mid-career researchers. Strong cases usually combine a current Professor or Associate Professor appointment, a PhD from a highly ranked global university, recent publications in highly ranked journals, a competitive h-index, national-level research grants and keynote roles at major international conferences.

How Priorities 1, 2 and 5 work

Priority 1 covers candidates from any sector who hold an international top-of-field award. Priority 2 covers candidates from any sector nominated by an expert Australian Government, State or Territory government agency; the agency completes Part A of Form 1000 and a supporting statement, both attached to the EOI. For these candidates a broader range of achievements may be considered, including top-of-field sports and arts awards, a track record as a significant angel investor or of leading a company to an IPO, promising entrepreneurial activity in Australia, and exceptional service to the Australian community. All other applications fall into Priority 5, the lowest.

Building the evidence

Evidence of recognition beyond the employer.

Decision makers look for evidence that the applicant's own work is recognised beyond the employer. Evidence should name the applicant or show their personal contribution; company awards and employer press releases that do not do so carry little weight. A senior role at an internationally reputed organisation and earnings at or above the high income threshold are recognised indicators in their own right; third-party evidence of impact, such as awards, citations, media coverage and expert references, makes them considerably stronger.

Evidence of achievement in the sector

Record who recognised the work, and where it was recognised.

Every indicator claimed in the Expression of Interest (EOI) must be supported by documents in the visa application, and the claims should remain consistent from EOI to lodgement. The EOI itself must include the passport bio-data page, a CV and evidence of the highest qualification. No information can be added after the EOI is submitted.

  • Media articles, interviews and industry references to the applicant's work
  • Case studies of products, projects or research the applicant led
  • Award certificates, grant letters and patent registrations
  • Publication records, citation metrics and a Google Scholar profile
  • Conference programmes confirming keynote or invited speaker roles
  • Employment contracts and payslips showing role and salary
  • Reference letters from recognised experts in the field
  • A statement of the applicant's plans and opportunities in Australia

How the process works

The NIV is invitation only.

A visa application cannot be lodged until the Department issues an invitation. Invitation rounds are held monthly. Between July and September 2026 the Department received 1,969 EOIs and issued 292 invitations: 216 to Tier One candidates (Priority 3) and 45 to Tier Two (Priority 4). An EOI stays active for two years. An invitation is not a pre-assessment: the application must be lodged within 60 days, which cannot be extended, and must prove the achievements the invitation was based on.

What an NIV assessment reviews

Three questions to settle before an EOI.

Before submitting an EOI, the record should be organised around three areas: sector fit, indicators and supporting proof. A gap identified at this stage can still be addressed; after invitation, only 60 days remain in which to lodge.

Sector fit

  • The priority sector the work belongs to
  • Tier One or Tier Two
  • Specialisation within the sector
  • The benefit of the work to Australia

Indicators

  • Awards, grants and patents
  • PhD, publications and h-index
  • Senior technical or management roles
  • Keynotes and invited speaking
  • Salary against the high income threshold (AUD 190,100)

Supporting proof

  • Media coverage and case studies
  • Expert references
  • An eligible Form 1000 nominator with a national reputation in the same area (not necessarily same field but this is recommended)
  • Family, health and character details

Frequently asked questions

Common NIV questions.

Short answers to the questions applicants ask most often. Visa settings change, so current requirements should be confirmed before lodging.

Do I need a job offer or employer sponsor?

No. The NIV requires neither employer sponsorship nor a job offer. Applicants must, however, show that they could find work or become established in their field in Australia, and a written job offer at or above the high income threshold may be relied on as an indicator of achievement.

Who can be my nominator?

The nominator must be an Australian citizen, an Australian permanent resident, an eligible New Zealand citizen or an Australian organisation, and must have a national reputation in the applicant's field. The nominator completes Form 1000 attesting to the applicant's record. The signed form, with evidence of the nominator's status and national reputation, must accompany the application at lodgement and cannot be changed afterwards. For nomination by an expert government agency (Priority 2), Part A of Form 1000 and the agency's supporting statement are attached earlier, to the EOI.

How many indicators do I need?

There is no fixed number in the Direction, which treats the indicators as guidance and asks the Department to weigh the record as a whole, looking for multiple achievements. As a working benchmark, we expect at least two indicators, whether the candidate is in a Tier One or a Tier Two sector, each backed by evidence of recognised achievement. A single award or milestone is unlikely to be enough on its own.

What if my field is not a priority sector?

An EOI may still be submitted, but candidates outside the priority sectors are considered after Priorities 1 to 4. Two routes apply to any sector: holders of an international top-of-field award are Priority 1, and candidates nominated on Form 1000 by an expert Australian Government, State or Territory government agency are Priority 2. Athletes, creatives, entrepreneurs and investors outside the priority sectors usually rely on the Priority 2 route.

How much does the visa cost?

There is no charge to submit an EOI. From 1 July 2026, the visa application charge is AUD 6,235 for the main applicant, plus AUD 3,120 for each additional applicant aged 18 or over and AUD 1,560 for each applicant under 18, and it is not refunded if the application is refused. A second instalment of AUD 4,890 is payable before grant for each applicant aged 18 or over who does not have functional English. Health examinations, police certificates, translations and biometrics are charged separately, as are professional fees if you engage an adviser.

How long does it take?

There are two stages. An EOI stays in the pool for up to two years, with no guarantee of an invitation and no progress updates from the Department. Once an application is lodged, processing time depends on the priority level, the completeness of the application, places available in the Migration Program, and health, character and security checks. Higher priority candidates are invited and processed first.
